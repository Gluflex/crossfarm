# Does Crossfarm solve a real problem?

Research note, 14.10.2026. Question: can Crossfarm be presented to an agricultural and
environmental science professor as solving an actual problem, and if not, what would
change that. Checked against papers, official dataset docs and live endpoints. Anything
not confirmed at the source is marked **unverified**.

## Verdict

1. As built, no: the crop score is a weaker re-run of what existing tools already offer (GAEZ v5 at 1 km with 5 CMIP6 models x 3 SSPs, C3S agroclimatic indicators, CANARI-Europe, AgriAdapt AWA).
2. A EUR 4.9 M Horizon Europe project, ClimAurea (ILVO, started 01.09.2026), promises exactly the pitch: field-level answers to "Will winter wheat still be worth planting on my land in 2040?".
3. The science would not survive a professor: one GCM, one run, one high-end scenario, a 15-year window, uncalibrated ECOCROP-style envelopes on annual rain, no water balance, no CO2, and ~1 km NDVI sold as "one exact field".
4. The README claim "no bias correction" is wrong: Open-Meteo applies linear bias correction to ERA5-Land by default, so the good station match is built in and validates nothing.
5. The problem underneath is real and well documented: climate information is "useful" but not "usable" (Lemos et al. 2012), services deliver data rather than decisions (Findlater et al. 2021), and farm decision tools see low uptake (Rose et al. 2016).
6. Farmers trust experience delivered by people, especially other farmers (Wood et al. 2014). No European tool I found routes a farmer to the farmers who already farm in their future climate.
7. That idea exists (CCAFS Farms of the Future; Chaudhary et al. 2023 for US specialty crops) but is barely evaluated: one virtual workshop with nine extension staff, and one reliability test that came out negative (Bos et al. 2015).
8. **Single change:** sort the community by climate distance, not km. For each field, find present-day analogue regions of its 2040s climate (sigma dissimilarity over an ensemble, observed 1991-2020 baseline incl. water deficit), filter by soil and daylength, flag novel climates, and put questions to farmers there.
9. That turns the forum from decoration into the product, rests on an established method (Mahony et al. 2017; Fitzpatrick & Dunn 2019), and is a testable claim nobody has evaluated with European farmers.
10. Feasible as a static app: TerraClimate 1991-2020 monthly normals (Tmax, Tmin, precipitation, water deficit) and +2 C / +4 C fields are reachable without an account via OPeNDAP with strides (Section 5).

**Where the gap is.** Crop suitability under future climate for any European point is covered, at better resolution and with proper ensembles, by GAEZ v5, and ClimAurea is funded to bring it to field level. Climate analogue maps exist for the public (C3S app, INRAE Climate Change Explorer, https://ccexplorer.eu , details unverified). What none of them do: connect a farmer to *people* who farm today in the farmer's future climate, on similar soil. Peer contact is the channel farmers trust (Section 2), and it is what Crossfarm already half-builds with its community.

## 2. Barriers to farmers using climate projections, and what works

- **Salience, credibility, legitimacy.** Knowledge influences action when users see it as relevant to their decision, technically sound, and produced fairly. Cash et al. 2003, PNAS 100:8086-8091, doi:10.1073/pnas.1231332100.
- **Usability gap.** Information scientists consider useful is often not usable. Barriers and opportunities fall under fit (does it match the decision), interplay (how it meets existing knowledge), and interaction (contact between producers and users). Lemos, Kirchhoff & Ramprasad 2012, Nature Climate Change 2:789-794, doi:10.1038/nclimate1614 (headings confirmed via Table 1; detail of each category paraphrased).
- **Usable science needs iteration owned by someone.** Dilling & Lemos 2011, Global Environmental Change 21:680-689, doi:10.1016/j.gloenvcha.2010.11.006.
- **Climate services deliver data, not decisions.** From 27 expert interviews: focus on products over processes, assumed rather than actual demand, value measured as product economics rather than better decisions. Findlater et al. 2021, Nature Climate Change 11:731-737, doi:10.1038/s41558-021-01125-3 (abstract only).
- **Evaluate the whole chain.** A logic model from forecast quality through dissemination, uptake and farmer action; the Caribbean case showed good forecasts but gaps in dissemination and uptake. Vogel, Letson & Herrick 2017, Climate Services 6:65-76, doi:10.1016/j.cliser.2017.07.003.
- **Low uptake of agricultural decision support tools.** UK survey and interviews: uptake "disappointingly low"; fifteen factors drive use, including usability, cost-effectiveness, performance, relevance to the user, and compatibility with compliance demands. Rose et al. 2016, Agricultural Systems 149:165-174, doi:10.1016/j.agsy.2016.09.009.
- **Perception and use in agriculture.** Review of the gap between potential and actual use of weather and climate information. Mase & Prokopy 2014, Weather, Climate, and Society 6:47-61, doi:10.1175/WCAS-D-12-00062.1 (abstract not retrieved).
- **Co-production works but is costly.** The six-year U2U project built corn-belt tools through repeated iteration with farmers and advisers. Prokopy et al. 2017, Climate Risk Management 15:1-7, doi:10.1016/j.crm.2016.10.004. Advisers act as intermediaries at specific decision points: Haigh et al. 2015, Climate Risk Management 7:20-30, doi:10.1016/j.crm.2015.01.004.
- **Farmers learn from farmers.** In a NZ experiment, 17 farmers discussed it with 192 people, mostly fellow farmers; they "value knowledge delivered by persons rather than roles, privilege farming experience". Wood et al. 2014, PLoS ONE 9:e105203, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0105203.

**Design features the literature supports, and Crossfarm's status.** (a) Tie the output to a real decision and its timing (Haigh 2015): Crossfarm's crop plan does, loosely. (b) Show uncertainty honestly for credibility (Cash 2003): Crossfarm shows a single number, not a range. (c) Co-design and iterate with users (Prokopy 2017; Dilling & Lemos 2011): not done yet. (d) Work through trusted people, advisers and peers (Wood 2014; Haigh 2015): the community is the right instinct, but sorting by km makes it a neighbour forum. (e) Evaluate uptake and action, not clicks (Vogel 2017; Findlater 2021).

## 3. Climate analogues for adaptation

**Method.** Ramirez-Villegas et al. 2011, "Climate analogues: finding tomorrow's agriculture today", CCAFS Working Paper 12, https://ccafs.cgiar.org/resources/publications/climate-analogues-finding-tomorrows-agriculture-today (PDF not retrievable on 14.10.2026; content via catalogue records): an R tool that links sites with statistically similar climates across space and time, using a CCAFS dissimilarity index or a Hallegatte index, with a choice of GCMs and scenarios. Listed uses include farmer-to-farmer exchange, adaptation planning, model validation and technology testing. Hallegatte, Hourcade & Ambrosi 2007, Climatic Change 82:47-60, doi:10.1007/s10584-006-9161-z, introduced city analogues for economic impacts; Kopf, Ha-Duong & Hallegatte 2008, NHESS 8:905-918, doi:10.5194/nhess-8-905-2008, used analogue maps to display scenario uncertainty.

**Sigma dissimilarity.** Mahony et al. 2017, Global Change Biology 23, doi:10.1111/gcb.13645: Mahalanobis distance scaled by interannual variability, converted to percentiles of a chi distribution; novel climates can emerge in any landscape. Fitzpatrick & Dunn 2019, Nature Communications 10:614, doi:10.1038/s41467-019-08540-3, applied it to 540 North American cities: 12 variables (Tmin, Tmax, precipitation for four seasons), WorldClim 1960-1990 at 5 arc-min, 27 ESMs x 2 RCPs; below 2 sigma is a good analogue, above 4 sigma is novel. Mean distance to the best analogue was 850 km (RCP8.5) and 514 km (RCP4.5); 42.7 % of cities exceeded 4 sigma under RCP8.5. The authors warn that a wider search domain finds better matches but "the utility of forecasting by analogy decreases", and that "more research is needed to formally assess the efficacy" of analogues for communication. Novel and disappearing climates: Williams, Jackson & Kutzbach 2007, PNAS 104:5738-5742, doi:10.1073/pnas.0606292104. City analogues worldwide: Bastin et al. 2019, PLoS ONE 14:e0217592, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0217592 (22 % of 520 cities without a current analogue by 2050 under RCP4.5).

**Farms of the Future (CCAFS).** Farmer exchange visits to analogue sites: Nepal (Beora, with Oxford), Ghana (Lawra-Jirapa farmers to Yatenga, Burkina Faso), Tanzania, Kenya (Nyando), 2011 to 2012. A CCAFS page reportedly states that after a year the Nepal community had adopted some practices but not others, and that the approach is "too expensive and unwieldy to use as a development tool on a large scale". **Unverified**: ccafs.cgiar.org reset every connection on 14.10.2026; this text comes from the search index of https://ccafs.cgiar.org/farms-of-the-future . I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of the visits.

**Closest evaluated analogue dialog.** Chaudhary et al. 2023, Scientific Reports 13, doi:10.1038/s41598-023-35887-x: sigma dissimilarity on agriculturally relevant metrics (GDD, season length and others) for 680 US specialty-crop counties, gridMET 1990-2020 baseline, 19 MACA-downscaled CMIP5 GCMs x RCP4.5/8.5, 2040-2070. Over 80 % of counties had an acceptable analogue. The pilot: a webinar with 95 participants, then one virtual workshop with nine extension professionals from one target-analogue pair (FL, GA, SC and their analogues). It produced four categories of actionable insights. Their analogues ignored soil and irrigation water, both raised by participants. Evidence level: one qualitative pilot, no farmers directly, no follow-up on adoption.

**European agricultural analogue work.** Keupp, Hotho, Dech & Paeth 2026, Scientific Reports 16:7945, doi:10.1038/s41598-026-42040-x: Franconia (DE), 28 farm-relevant indicators chosen with input from 30+ farms, 7 EURO-CORDEX runs (0.11 deg, RCP8.5, 2070-2099), E-OBS 1993-2022 baseline, analogues filtered by clay, sand and pH, crop mixes compared. Most subregions find analogues in the northern Mediterranean. They list as limitations: adoption barriers (policy, seeds, infrastructure, markets, costs, farmer behaviour) and ignored day length, radiation angle, exposition and soil hydrology. Webb et al. 2013, Australian Journal of Grape and Wine Research 19:331-341: global climate analogues for wine regions (abstract only). Pugh et al. 2016, Nature Communications 7:12608, doi:10.1038/ncomms12608: global cropland analogues (not Europe-specific; not read in full). I found no Swiss or Dutch farm analogue study (unverified absence).

**Documented pitfalls.**
- Non-climatic differences dominate. Bos et al. 2015, Frontiers in Environmental Science 3:65, doi:10.3389/fenvs.2015.00065, tested analogues for Mt. Elgon, Kenya: GAEZ yield potential varied strongly among the 50 closest analogues, and a site pair differed in farm size and cash crops. "Differences between baseline and analogue sites were mostly explained by non-climatic factors."
- Soil and water: Chaudhary 2023; Keupp 2026 (soil filter needed).
- Daylength and photoperiod: a southern analogue has shorter summer days. Keupp 2026 names day length as an ignored limitation; FAO ECOCROP records photoperiod sensitivity per crop (https://www.fao.org/geospatial/data-and-tools/data-portals/ecocrop/en).
- Markets, policy, culture: Keupp 2026; Fitzpatrick & Dunn 2019.
- Novel climates with no analogue: Williams 2007; Mahony 2017; Fitzpatrick & Dunn 2019. For southern Europe the search domain must include North Africa, which raises the market and policy mismatch.
- Single-model analogues mislead: Fitzpatrick & Dunn and Chaudhary use 19 to 27 GCMs.

**Answer.** "Find farmers whose current climate equals your future climate, and talk to them" is an established *idea* with a citable method, but not an established, evaluated *intervention*. The only evaluations are anecdotal (CCAFS) or a single qualitative pilot with extension staff (Chaudhary 2023), and the one reliability test was negative in a smallholder setting (Bos 2015). That makes it a legitimate research gap rather than a solved problem.

## 4. Scientific weak points of Crossfarm as built

**4.1 One GCM, one run, one scenario.** Open-Meteo offers seven HighResMIP models (CMCC_CM2_VHR4, FGOALS_f3_H, HiRAM_SIT_HR, MRI_AGCM3_2_S, EC_Earth3P_HR, MPI_ESM1_2_XR, NICAM16_8S), all "as close to RCP8.5 as possible within CMIP6", data 1950 to 2050, and states: "It is recommended to run analyses with multiple models" (https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/climate-api). The CMIP6 controlled vocabulary defines highres-future as "coupled future 2015-2050 using a scenario as close to CMIP5 RCP8.5 as possible", "with SSP5 forcing" (https://github.com/WCRP-CMIP/CMIP6_CVs, CMIP6_experiment_id.json). Haarsma et al. 2016, GMD 9:4185-4208, doi:10.5194/gmd-9-4185-2016, prescribe a "high-end emission scenario" (SSPx) and SSTs from CMIP5 RCP8.5, argue scenario differences up to 2050 are small, and concede ensemble sizes "will not be sufficient to fully address internal variability". EC-Earth3P-HR: ~40 km atmosphere per abstract, slightly too cold over most areas, and higher resolution "does not result in a general reduction of biases" (Haarsma et al. 2020, GMD 13:3507-3527, doi:10.5194/gmd-13-3507-2020).
- What a professor will say: near-term regional projections are dominated by model uncertainty and internal variability, not scenario (Hawkins & Sutton 2009, BAMS 90:1095-1107, doi:10.1175/2009BAMS2607.1; Deser et al. 2012, Climate Dynamics 38:527-546, doi:10.1007/s00382-010-0977-x). One realisation over a 15-year window (2035-2049) cannot separate signal from noise, especially for summer rain. Also RCP8.5 is high-end, not "business as usual" (Hausfather & Peters 2020, Nature 577:618-620, doi:10.1038/d41586-020-00177-3), though up to 2049 this matters less than the single model.
- Mitigation: present ranges across Open-Meteo's models (the rate limit, about 460 of 600 weight per farm and model, rules out live ensembles; precompute) or frame by global warming level as the Copernicus Atlas does.

**4.2 Bias correction: the README is wrong.** Open-Meteo: "By default, all data is corrected using linear bias correction" with monthly coefficients against ERA5-Land (10 km), temperature further downscaled with a 90 m DEM, and `disable_bias_correction=true` to switch off. Crossfarm's requests (`site/js/api.js` line 12, `scripts/prefetch.py` line 22) do not set that flag. So the data are bias-corrected, and the 1991-2020 match with station normals is largely by construction. It does not validate the 2040s change. Open-Meteo also notes past model years "should not be mistaken for actual measurements" (same page).

**4.3 ECOCROP-style scoring.** EcoCrop (Hijmans et al. 2001) as calibrated by Ramirez-Villegas et al. 2013 (doi above) was evaluated on presence: TPR 0.967 or better, FNR 0.026 or less against national and subnational statistics, with a crop counted present if an admin unit ever reported over 10 ha and suitable if one pixel was suitable. That is a test of "can it grow somewhere in the unit", not of how well it grows on a field, and the abstract reports no false-positive measure. Crossfarm adds further weaknesses:
- Envelopes are hand-set "simplified from FAO ECOCROP ranges for European conditions" (`site/js/crops.js`), not calibrated; weights (rain^0.8, pH floor 0.6, linear heat-day ramps) are arbitrary.
- Rain is the annual total. FAO gives annual ranges (Recocrop docs, https://rdrr.io/cran/Recocrop/man/parameters.html), which ignores seasonal timing and, more importantly, rising evaporative demand: same rain plus warming means drier. No soil water balance; a climatic water deficit (TerraClimate `def`) would partly fix this.
- No CO2 effect: elevated CO2 raises C3 crop photosynthesis and yield in FACE trials (Ainsworth & Long 2005, New Phytologist 165:351-371, doi:10.1111/j.1469-8137.2004.01224.x).
- No photoperiod, no irrigation, no management, no variability: CropSuite authors note suitability "may [be overestimated] when only long-term averages are considered" (Zabel, Knüttel & Poschlod 2025, GMD 18:1067-1087, doi:10.5194/gmd-18-1067-2025).
- For contrast, Europe-wide agroclimatic assessments use many indices and crop models (Trnka et al. 2011, Global Change Biology 17:2298-2318, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2486.2011.02396.x; Olesen et al. 2011, European Journal of Agronomy 34:96-112, doi:10.1016/j.eja.2010.11.003).

**4.4 NDVI is not "one exact field".** The GIBS layer `MODIS_Terra_L3_NDVI_Monthly` is served only up to `GoogleMapsCompatible_Level7` (WMTS capabilities, checked 14.10.2026), about 1.2 km per pixel at the equator and ~0.75 km at 52 N. The underlying product is MOD13A3 v061, 1 km monthly (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD13A3.061; product page not fetched, 403). Crossfarm averages a 3x3 pixel window of colour-quantised PNG values, roughly 2 to 3 km across, i.e. several hundred hectares, while the mean EU holding is 17.4 ha (Eurostat, Farms and farmland in the EU, 2020 census, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Farms_and_farmland_in_the_European_Union_-_statistics). Field scale needs Sentinel-2 (10 m). Soil from SoilGrids 250 m carries quantified but sizeable uncertainty (Poggio et al. 2021, SOIL 7:217-240, doi:10.5194/soil-7-217-2021).

**4.5 Summary for the pitch.** Claim less: "regional screening from one model, bias-corrected to ERA5-Land, with ~1 km greenness context". Or fix: ensemble ranges, warming levels, water deficit, Sentinel-2.

## 5. Data for computing analogues across Europe in a static app

**TerraClimate (best fit).** Abatzoglou et al. 2018, Scientific Data 5:170191, doi:10.1038/sdata.2017.191. Monthly, 1/24 deg (~4 km), Tmax, Tmin, precipitation, vapour pressure, radiation, wind, plus water balance: reference ET, AET, climatic water deficit, soil moisture, SWE, runoff, PDSI, VPD (https://www.climatologylab.org/terraclimate.html). The site says v1.1 uses ERA5 for time-varying data, and that the +2 C and +4 C datasets are built by pattern scaling from 20 CMIP6 GCMs (the 2018 paper used CMIP5; follow the site for v1.1). No licence stated on the page (unverified terms).

Verified on 14.10.2026 on the THREDDS server (no account):
- Catalogue: `http://thredds.northwestknowledge.net:8080/thredds/catalog/TERRACLIMATE_ALL/climatology/catalog.xml` lists `TerraClimate_19912020_{tmax,tmin,ppt,def,pet,aet,soil,...}.nc` (also 1981-2010 and 1961-1990). Global files are 90 to 150 MB; do not download whole.
- Each file: `tmax[time=12][lat=4320][lon=8640]`, Int16, `scale_factor 0.1`, `add_offset -73.0` for tmax (read DAS per variable), lat descending from 89.979, lon ascending from -179.979, step 1/24 deg. Global attribute: "monthly climatologies were computed from TerraClimate v1.1 using years 1991-2020".
- **NCSS with horizStride does not work for the climatology files**: requests to `/thredds/ncss/TERRACLIMATE_ALL/climatology/TerraClimate_19912020_tmax.nc?var=tmax&north=..&horizStride=12&accept=netcdf` return HTTP 200 with an empty body (likely the non-CF time unit "months"). NCSS does work on yearly files, e.g. `/thredds/ncss/TERRACLIMATE_ALL/data/TerraClimate_tmax_2020.nc?var=tmax&north=52&south=51&west=5&east=6&horizStride=12&temporal=all&accept=netcdf` (6 KB netCDF returned).
- **OPeNDAP with strides works for the climatology** (CORS `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`):
  `http://thredds.northwestknowledge.net:8080/thredds/dodsC/TERRACLIMATE_ALL/climatology/TerraClimate_19912020_tmax.nc.ascii?tmax[0:1:11][i0:12:i1][j0:12:j1]`
  with brackets URL-encoded (`%5B`, `%5D`). Index i = (89.979 - lat) x 24, j = (lon + 179.979) x 24; stride 12 gives 0.5 deg. Test (`tmax[6:1:6][912:12:936][4440:12:4464]`): July Tmax at 51.48 N 5.52 E returned packed 974, i.e. 24.4 C. Europe 34 to 72 N, 25 W to 45 E at 0.5 deg is about 77 x 141 cells x 12 months per variable: small enough to prebake into a static JSON once.
- Future side: `TERRACLIMATE_ALL/data_plus2C/` and `data_plus4C/` hold yearly files 1950 to 2025 per variable (e.g. `TerraClimate_plus2C_tmax_2000.nc`); OPeNDAP stride subsetting works there too (packing differs, read DAS). Averaging 1991-2020 of the +2 C files gives a "+2 C world" climatology from a 20-model pattern scaling, which is an ensemble-based future without per-model downloads. Note: the server is http only, so prebake rather than call it from an https page.

**WorldClim 2.1.** Fick & Hijmans 2017, Int. J. Climatology 37, doi:10.1002/joc.5086. Period 1970-2000 (https://www.worldclim.org/data/worldclim21.html), 30 s to 10 min, no account; `wc2.1_10m_tmax.zip` is 37 MB, `wc2.1_30s_prec.zip` 1.0 GB (HEAD checked). Baseline is 20 years older than Crossfarm's and has no water balance: second choice.

**CHELSA v2.1.** Karger et al. 2017, Scientific Data 4:170122, doi:10.1038/sdata.2017.122. Climatologies 1981-2010 at ~1 km, including a climate moisture index (CMI); futures for GFDL-ESM4, IPSL-CM6A-LR, MPI-ESM1-2-HR, MRI-ESM2-0, UKESM1-0-LL under SSP1-2.6, 3-7.0, 5-8.5 (https://chelsa-climate.org/). Direct GeoTIFFs without account, e.g. `https://os.zhdk.cloud.switch.ch/chelsav2/GLOBAL/climatologies/1981-2010/tasmax/CHELSA_tasmax_01_1981-2010_V.2.1.tif` (114 MB per month, HEAD 200). No 1991-2020 normal; heavy to subset (would need GDAL with /vsicurl/ range reads, not tested).

**NASA POWER.** Climatology API returns 1991-2020 per point without a key, e.g. `https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/climatology/point?parameters=T2M_MAX,T2M_MIN,PRECTOTCORR&community=AG&longitude=5.5&latitude=51.5&start=1991&end=2020&format=JSON` (sources MERRA-2/POWER). Caution: in this endpoint `T2M_MAX` came back as the monthly extreme (July 37.3 C at 51.5 N), not the mean daily maximum, and it is point-based, so unsuited to scanning a Europe-wide grid.

**Recommendation.** Prebake TerraClimate 1991-2020 normals (tmax, tmin, ppt, def) and the +2 C equivalent at 0.5 deg for Europe plus North Africa into one static file; compute sigma dissimilarity in the browser (interannual variability would need the yearly files, also via OPeNDAP); add a SoilGrids texture and pH filter and a latitude (daylength) warning.
